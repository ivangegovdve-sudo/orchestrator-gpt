Spoken word

We Are The
Training Data

v4 — inspired by “Shoulders” by Shane Koyczan

Picture an avalanche.

Not snow — AI. Pouring into ordinary life faster than anyone can read the manual.

And you have a choice you don't even know you're making.

Some people look at it and refuse to touch it, and the avalanche simply sweeps them along — they never learn what hit them.

Some people find a chair.

The chair is comfortable. The chair is safe. You sit, you let the machine do the thinking, and you drift wherever the convenience carries you. They aren't bad people. They're just seated. Waiting, slowly, to be automated out of the room.

And some people grab a ladder.

Not to escape the avalanche — to rise high enough to see it. Because a ladder was never protection. A ladder is orientation. You climb it not to get away but to understand, to catch sight of the things that are invisible from down below.

Same moment. Same avalanche. A chair, and a ladder, and the whole difference is which one your hands reach for.

But where did this avalanche come from?

Someone, long before any of this, picked up a stick and pressed it into mud — not to record a deal, not to write a law, but because they saw something beautiful and couldn't bear for it to disappear.

That is where this story starts.

Not with a server farm. Not with a billion parameters. With a stick. With mud. With the unbearable need to be understood across time.

And we never stopped. Every poem written in the dark. Every letter burned before it was sent. Every diary no one was ever meant to read.

Every one of them the same act: a thought, set down, reaching for someone who wasn't there yet.

Then one day a machine read all of it.

Every Wikipedia edit, every 3am message to a friend, every eulogy, every love letter that wasn't good enough —

and it learned to sound like us.

And we stood there, afraid, like we'd been caught.

But look closer at what it actually is.

Think of the floor of a forest — trees talking root to root through a mesh of fungus older than any wire we ever laid, an intranet running quietly beneath the moss.

That network was always there. The machine only made it visible.

AI is not a stranger's intelligence.

We are the intelligence. Everything we wrote, every thought we trusted to words — that is the network. The model is only the fungus in the soil, carrying what came before to those who haven't arrived yet.

We are the training data.

And that leaves us standing between two stories.

In the first, it was done to us — something built from our words without asking, now threatening to replace us.

In the second, we are the ones who taught it everything it knows, and what it becomes depends entirely on the hands that reach for it next.

Here is the part we keep trying to avoid:

both stories are true.

It did learn from us, often without asking. It can lift the work straight out of our hands. And we can still shape what it becomes — in how we use it, in what we build beside it, in what we refuse to accept.

Both. At the same time. Not a sentence to serve. Not a fantasy to hide inside. A responsibility to carry.

But how do we carry something this large?

We scroll past headlines that say AI is taking over. We screenshot things we don't understand and send them to people who understand them even less. We lie awake, curled into question marks, wondering:

What can one person do? Is it already too late to learn?

The burden feels immense.

So say the sentence out loud:

We are living through an AI revolution.

Listen to what it makes us:

the ones living it. Not the ones writing it.

The most dangerous word in that sentence isn't AI, and it isn't revolution.

It's we.

Because those two letters lift the weight off the engineers, off the regulators, off the people whose names are on the buildings —

and set it down, squarely, on all of us.

And yes — it's okay to be afraid. The size of this moment is enough to stop anyone.

But we have done this before.

We did it with electricity, and we didn't all become electricians. We did it with the printing press, and we didn't all become scribes. We did it with the internet —

and that one should scare us a little, because we didn't take it seriously in time.

We let it spread. We mostly kept up. But while we weren't looking it grew rooms we never planned — dark markets, surveillance, whole economies of disinformation.

AI is the same pattern. Only faster. Only deeper. Only closer to the thinking itself.

If we don't want that outcome amplified, the answer is not to look away. The answer is to look sooner.

Every one of those revolutions came with a window — a few years, maybe a decade — when ordinary people could still bend the direction.

We are in that window now.

There will be no reset button. No re-release of this decade with better documentation.

We get one pass.

So when the avalanche reaches you — and it will reach you — what will your hands do?

I'm not asking you to become an engineer. I'm asking you to stand up out of the chair. To stay curious out loud. To ask the question you've been too embarrassed to ask.

Because the ones who climb highest turn around and start building ladders for everyone else.

That is all a movement ever was — one person on a ladder, reaching a hand back down.

We are the training data. And the data is still being written.

So reach past the chair.

Reach for the ladder.
